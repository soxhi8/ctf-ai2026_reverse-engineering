**ROP (Return-Oriented Programming)** — more specifically, in this case a simple variant called **ret2win**.


Since NX prevents executing shellcode injected onto the stack, you can't just jump to your own shellcode. Instead, you redirect execution to *existing* executable code already in the binary — here, that's the `win()` function itself. You overflow the buffer, overwrite the saved return address with `win()`'s address, and when `vuln()` returns, it "returns" into `win()` instead, which runs `system("cat flag.txt")` for you.


Full ROP chains (chaining multiple gadgets) are typically needed when there's no direct target function like `win()` to jump to — here it's the simplest case of that same NX-bypass family.
